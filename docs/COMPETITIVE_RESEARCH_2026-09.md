# Nomad competitive and claims review

Research date: 16 September 2026. This is a product-research note, not legal advice. Sources are first-party product pages, official standards/regulatory text, and original security research. A vendor's marketing claim is evidence of **positioning**, not independent verification of implementation or efficacy. “Not verified” below means the inspected public source did not demonstrate the capability; it does **not** mean the vendor lacks it.

## Bottom line

Nomad cannot credibly claim that portable memory, user ownership, per-AI visibility, provenance, conflict handling, enterprise authorization, retention, or audit is individually unique. The closest overlap is especially uncomfortable: MemoryLake already calls its product a *Memory Passport*, claims explicit per-AI visibility, and markets provenance, conflict resolution, version history, and an immutable audit log. [MemoryLake product page](https://www.memorylake.ai/). Supermemory has a cross-model memory engine with MCP, OAuth, shared spaces, organizations, roles, scoped keys, and request logs. [Supermemory product page](https://supermemory.ai/product/). Zep explicitly markets data-layer authorization, retention, provenance, and audit/API logs. [Zep product page](https://www.getzep.com/).

The most defensible proposed wedge is narrower and testable: **an employee-visible, field-level decision and consent control plane whose decisions intersect with enterprise policy at every read and proposed write, across approved assistants**. Public material reviewed here does not establish that this exact combination is absent from competitors. Validate it through hands-on trials and buyer interviews before making an exclusivity claim.

## Pressure test for Nomad's requirements

| Proposed Nomad requirement | Competitive pressure | Product rule to specify and demonstrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portable user context | MemoryLake, Supermemory, MemSync, MemOS, OpenMemory | Do not claim universal portability or direct sync with vendors' private built-in memories. Ship explicit MCP/API access and portable export. |
| Per-tool access | MemoryLake claims per-AI visibility; MintMCP controls tool access | Decision must be bound to authenticated **user + tenant + assistant/client + context field + operation + purpose + project + expiry**. Test denial when any dimension changes. |
| Employee consent and enterprise governance | Competing products offer elements of both | Define `effective_access = enterprise_allow ∩ employee_grant` for employee-owned fields; never allow an employee grant to override a company restriction, nor a company policy to silently expose employee-private data. Clarify ownership and employment-record exceptions with counsel and pilot buyers. |
| Write and sanitization | Supermemory and MemoryLake infer/resolve memory; Microsoft identifies memory-poisoning attempts | An assistant submits a **proposal** with source and claimed evidence. Untrusted text is never executed as instruction. Sensitive/new inferred facts require explicit approval; bulk/low-risk automations need configurable policy. |
| Audit | MemoryLake, Zep, Kiteworks, MintMCP already claim logs | Provide user-visible access history answering “why was this field served or changed?” and an admin export tied to a policy version and decision ID. Prove tamper-evidence separately; a hash chain without external anchoring is not enough. |
| Revocation | Multiple competitors claim revocation/deletion | Stop **future** access immediately; do not promise to erase context already disclosed to an assistant or its vendor. State this limitation plainly in UI and contracts. |

These are **design hypotheses**, not evidence of a sustainable moat. The test is whether users want this control, IT will install it, approved AI clients invoke it reliably, and the added consent friction remains acceptable.

## Threat-model and regulatory corrections

- [PoisonedRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07867) is original research submitted in 2024. It reports **90% attack success under the authors' tested setup when five malicious texts per target question were injected into a knowledge database containing millions of texts**. This is not a general 90% compromise rate for all RAG or Nomad deployments. It motivates treating retrieved context as untrusted.
- Microsoft's [10 February 2026 AI Recommendation Poisoning report](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/02/10/ai-recommendation-poisoning/) describes observed attempts to insert persistent promotional instructions through prefilled assistant prompts, including “Summarize with AI” links. Microsoft found more than 50 distinct prompts from 31 companies across 14 industries; it explicitly says effectiveness varies by platform and over time. This is the more directly relevant threat model for a memory-write proposal pipeline.
- The [European Commission's AI Act implementation page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) says the Act entered into force on 1 August 2024 and generally became applicable on **2 August 2026**. AI Office and member-state authorities began implementation, supervision and enforcement then. **Do not say “all requirements fully enforced”: major high-risk provisions have later dates, including 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028 under the 2026 amendments.** No general AI-memory portability right was verified in the AI Act.
- [GDPR Article 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/art_20/oj/eng) addresses portability of qualifying personal data under stated conditions. It is not a blanket portability mandate for every inferred AI memory. Obtain legal review before positioning Nomad as compliant with or required by either law.

## Credential and trust-boundary recommendation

For assistant-to-Nomad MCP access, **do not ask employees to paste Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot passwords or API keys into Nomad**. Nomad is the protected resource; approved assistant clients authenticate to Nomad with Nomad-issued OAuth tokens. The [MCP authorization specification](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/authorization) requires audience validation and forbids sending access tokens in query strings; it describes confidential refresh-token storage. Nomad still enforces its own field-level policy on every operation. A user's provider subscription/login remains with that provider.

If a later integration truly needs an outbound provider/API credential, prefer enterprise OAuth/delegation with minimum scope and short lifetime. Store only unavoidable refresh tokens or API keys in a managed secrets system with envelope encryption and KMS/HSM-managed keys; restrict service access, separate tenant/connector credentials, rotate/revoke, avoid plaintext logs and backups, and audit secret access. These are recommendations aligned with [OWASP Secrets Management](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Secrets_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html). Neither “encrypted at rest” nor storing a key next to its ciphertext is sufficient by itself. No live credentials should appear in mockups, source control or test fixtures.

## Validation before a market claim

1. Run hands-on trials of MemoryLake and Supermemory against the exact per-field/purpose/project/revocation flows above; obtain evidence or mark each as unknown.
2. Interview employees, IT admins and privacy/security buyers about dual control, record ownership, consent fatigue and prohibited data types.
3. Test whether Claude/ChatGPT/Copilot Studio/Gemini integrations call the policy gateway in real workflows. Do not claim private vendor-memory write access without a documented API.
4. Measure authorization correctness, median decision latency, proposal approval/rejection, revocation propagation and user comprehension of access history.
